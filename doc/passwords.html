Passwords

Passwords keeps your logins, passkeys, one-time codes, cards and notes on your own server, encrypted on your own devices. Your phone or your browser seals each item before it is sent, so the server, whoever runs it and a stolen backup see only sealed text: no site, no user name, no password. The one exception is an item you choose to let your agent use.

Setting it up

Open Passwords on the dashboard or in the iPhone app and choose a passphrase. It is separate from your TeaNode sign-in and never leaves your device. Setup also makes a recovery key: print it or save it somewhere safe. A device asks for the passphrase once; after that the iPhone opens Passwords with Face ID, and Chrome keeps it open for as long as you choose.

If you lose both the passphrase and the recovery key, nobody can open your Passwords, the operator included. The only way on is to erase it and start again, and the setup screen says so.

What it holds

  • Logins: a user name, a password, the sites it is for, a one-time code and passkeys.
  • Cards: kept, shown and copied when you need them. Nothing fills a card for you.
  • Notes: anything else you want kept sealed, such as the Wi-Fi at the cabin.

Every change keeps the version before it, and a deleted item can be restored for a while.

Where it fills

  • Chrome. The TeaNode extension fills a login on its site, offers to save one after you sign in, makes up strong passwords, and makes and uses passkeys.
  • The iPhone. Turn on TeaNode in Settings, General, AutoFill & Passwords, and Safari, Chrome and other apps offer its logins, passkeys and one-time codes. A passkey sign-in on a computer, through its QR code, can use it too.
  • The command line. teanode passwords lists, shows, copies, adds and edits items, and makes up passwords.

Bringing them in

teanode passwords import pass reads a pass store, one-time codes and sites included. teanode passwords import csv <file> reads an export from Chrome, Apple Passwords or Bitwarden. Both skip what you already have, and teanode passwords export takes everything out again.

Sharing

Share an item with one person in a circle or with the whole circle. It is sealed on your device for each of them, so only their devices can open it. They can fill it, copy it and sign in with its passkey; only you change it. Stop sharing and the item gets a new key, so what they kept cannot open the next version.

Each person has a safety code, eight groups of four digits worked out from their key. If you want to be sure the server gave you the right key for somebody, compare codes with them in person or on a call. Nothing waits for it, and if a person's key ever changes, your devices tell you.

Your agent

Your agent cannot read Passwords. For one login at a time you can turn on Let the agent use this, and then it can sign in with that login for you:

  • only on that login's own sites, checked by the browser, never by what the model says;
  • in its own browser or in your Chrome tab, with the password, one-time code or passkey put into the page by TeaNode, never shown to the model;
  • only in a conversation you started or a schedule you wrote yourself. A run that a mail, a webhook or a chat app started cannot use it.

Each use is recorded on the item, with the site. A note you turn on is read by the model in full, since that is what you chose. A card can never be turned on, and nobody's agent can use an item somebody else shared with you.

The agent can also make up a password when you ask, but for a login you will keep, make it with the generator in Passwords, which no model sees.

On the command line it is teanode passwords; teanode passwords --help lists every command.

  • Coding sessions: Your Claude Code and Codex sessions in herdr, on every computer you attached: your agent reads them, types into them where you can see, and brings you their questions to answer from anywhere.
  • Memory in coding sessions: Claude Code and Codex shown what your agent knows about the checkout they run in: the project page and the last session when one starts, recall kept to the project before each prompt, and the transcript read within a minute.
  • Model Context Protocol: TeaNode on both ends of the protocol: answering it, so your editor can use your agent's tools and ask it questions, and connecting out to servers of your own.
  • Finance: Your banks, cards and brokerages linked through Plaid or SimpleFIN: transactions in your own categories, budgets that say where the month is heading, savings targets, net worth, and a tax year put together from your paystubs, W-2s, 1099s and 1098s, all kept in your own database.

  • Photos: Photos and videos on your own server: backed up from the iPhone in the background, found by place, date or what is in them, faces grouped into people you name, and albums shared with your circle.
  • Location: Visits to named places recorded by the iPhone app, a map of your day, where you are shared with the people in your circle, and what leaves the server to name a place.
  • Passwords: Logins, passkeys, one-time codes, cards and notes, encrypted on your own devices: filled in Chrome and on the iPhone, shared with your circle, and used by your agent only on the logins you turn on.
  • The command line: The two programs: the server and the few things only its host can do, and the client that administers it from anywhere through the API.
