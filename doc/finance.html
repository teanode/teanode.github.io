Finance

You link the institutions you hold money with (a bank, a card issuer, a brokerage, a lender), and the server keeps your accounts, transactions and balances in its own database. On top of those it keeps your net worth over time, your own spending categories, budgets and savings targets. Your agent reads and changes all of it, so you can ask it how the month is going in the same conversation where you ask about your mail.

Linking an institution

Two providers sign in to institutions for the server. The operator decides which ones are offered (agent.finance.offeredProviders in the configuration).

SimpleFIN needs nothing from the operator. You pay for the SimpleFIN Bridge yourself, make a setup token there, and paste it on the agent page's Finance tab or into teanode finance link-simplefin. The server claims it once and keeps what it gets back. The bridge holds about ninety days of history, so that is how far back a new link reaches.

Plaid needs the operator's developer account (agent.finance.plaid), because Plaid deals with operators, not with each person. You link through Plaid's own window, which opens on one dashboard page; that page is the only one allowed to load Plaid's script. When the bank wants you to sign in again, the finance source stops and asks you to, and signing in repairs the same link instead of making a new one. If the operator turns on investments, a brokerage linked through Plaid brings in its holdings and trades as well.

Already have a connection made elsewhere? You can bring it in instead of linking again: a Plaid credential made with this server's Plaid keys, or a SimpleFIN access address you already claimed. The provider is asked first, and nothing is made unless it answers.

A new link syncs within a minute, then every six hours.

Accounts no provider reaches

Some cards and banks are reached by neither provider, or only let their holder export a file. Those come in as imported statements, which need nothing from the operator.

A statement file. Export an OFX file (.ofx, .qfx or .qbo) and upload it under Accounts on the Finance page, run teanode finance import-statement, or mail it to your own statement import address, which the same page shows. A phone's banking app can usually share the export straight into a mail. Transactions already imported are updated rather than added again, so overlapping files count nothing twice, and your agent tells you in the main conversation which account the statement went into and how many transactions it brought.

Screenshots. An app that shows the transactions but exports nothing can be photographed. Send the screenshots to your agent in the drawer; it reads each row, and the import checks them before anything is written: running balances must chain from row to row and each month must come to its printed total, or nothing is imported and the row that does not add up is named. Only the rows the account does not hold already are added, and the account is found by the last digits it shows, so a later file or later screenshots land in the same account.

Accounts imported either way can be renamed, given the last digits of their number when the statement shows none, or deleted with their transactions.

What you get

Transactions, filed by you. Every transaction goes under one of your own spending categories. It is filed by the first of these that has an answer: you, a spending rule you made ("anything from Corner Grocer is groceries"), the provider's own category, and last a model. The model is sent the merchant, the description, the amount and the provider's category, never an account number. Two categories are built in: Transfer, for money moving between your own accounts and paying off a card, which counts as neither spending nor income, and Other, for what fits none of yours. A transaction with no category is one still to be decided, and the Transactions list can show just those.

Many at once. Choose transactions with the box at the start of each row (shift chooses a run, Select all every one the filters match, on every page) and give them a category together. Tick the box beside it and the same choice is saved as spending rules: one per merchant, each listed with how many transactions it matches and how many others it would change before anything is saved.

What a transaction was for. Write a note on any transaction ("party supplies"), or let your agent write one. It changes yours only when you ask it to in a conversation.

Receipts. A receipt is stored line by line as printed, checked against its printed totals, and matched to the charge it explains. Photograph one into the chat, upload a photo or a screenshot on the transaction itself, or let the agent read one that arrived as mail. A receipt is matched without asking only to the one charge of exactly its amount that shares a word of the merchant; anything less certain is offered to you. One recorded before its charge arrives waits, and is matched when the charge comes in. The photo sits above the lines, and you can zoom into it. The Receipts tab lists them all, searchable by what was bought, so "when did I last buy coffee beans" has an answer.

Duplicates. Some brokerages report one account-level fee on every account of a connection. Those mirrored copies are found, marked and left out of every total, and you can say a copy is a real charge of its own.

Ask about one. A transaction's details end with Ask the agent, which opens the drawer with that transaction attached to your question.

Budgets that say where the month is heading. A budget is a monthly amount for a spending category. Each one shows what has gone so far and where the month is heading: the spending so far, the charges that come every month and have not come yet, and the rest at this month's pace. It is under, on track, at risk or over. An income category can have a budget too, the income you expect each month, and it reads behind, on track or ahead. A budget's row opens its transactions for the month. Spending has a year view too: the year's totals, a chart of its months, and budgets and saving for the year from each month's own figures, with where the year is heading.

Saving. For any month: what you expected to save (income budgets less spending budgets), what you have saved so far, and where the month is heading.

Savings targets. An amount to save by a day, with what it needs each month from now on. A target can count money not spent, net worth gained since it started, or what chosen accounts and assets are worth (a whole brokerage account counts every holding, including ones bought later).

Net worth. Every account becomes an asset with a value each day. You add the rest yourself: a house, a car, a loan. A car or a house can be estimated by your agent from the web once a month, with the range and the pages it used, but only for an asset where you allowed it. Accounts the providers cannot reach can be read by your agent from a connected server on a daily schedule.

More than one currency. Every amount keeps its currency, and totals are converted at the European Central Bank's rate for each amount's own day into a reporting currency you choose. A currency the bank does not publish is left out of the total, and the total says so.

Alerts. At 80 percent of a budget, at risk, over, or a savings target falling behind, your agent tells you once a month, through the same alerts as mail (quiet hours and the daily limit included). A spending category can be muted on its own.

Taxes

Paystubs, W-2s, 1099s and 1098s are read out of the file you hand in, a PDF or a photo, and kept to get ready for a tax return. Hand them in on the Finance page's Taxes tab, share them from your phone, run teanode finance read-tax-document, or mail them to your own tax document address, which the Taxes tab shows; a payroll mail forwarded there works the same. Your agent tells you in the main conversation what arrived.

What is kept. A paystub is stored line by line as printed: each earning, each tax and each deduction, with the employer's part beside yours. A W-2 keeps its boxes. A 1099-INT, 1099-DIV or 1099-B (often all three in one brokerage statement) keeps its totals, and a 1098 its mortgage interest. Before a model reads anything, every number shaped like a taxpayer number is masked to its last four digits, only those four are stored, and the file is kept without its text.

What is checked. A paystub has to add up: gross pay less the taxes and your deductions is take-home pay. A W-2's Social Security and Medicare are checked against its wages, and set beside the year's last paystub. A paystub that does not follow on from the one before, a gap of more than a pay period and a half, a W-2 not in once the year is over, and Social Security withheld past the year's maximum across employers (which comes back on the return) are all pointed out.

The year. The tax year puts it together per person and employer: the W-2 where there is one, else the latest paystub's year to date, carried to December 31 at your pay cadence, with retirement contributions stopping at the year's limit. Before the 1099s come, interest and dividends are previewed from your linked accounts, leaving out IRAs, HSAs and the other accounts whose income is not taxed.

The estimate. The tax owed is set beside what was withheld: federal income tax by the year's brackets, itemizing when mortgage interest, state and local taxes up to the cap and gifts beat the standard deduction, qualified dividends and long-term gains at their own rates, the child tax credit and the child and dependent care credit, and Social Security and Medicare. What no document says (children, dependent care paid, a spouse's pay that has no W-2 here, gifts, property tax) goes in the year's settings. The filing status is yours to set; without it, two people on the documents means married filing jointly. It is a start for a return, not the return: state tax is taken as withheld, and credits other than those are not counted.

Three ways in

The same operations, under the same names, in three places:

  • The dashboard. The Finance page, /finance, has Spending, Transactions, Receipts, Accounts, Budgets, Net worth, Savings targets and Taxes. The agent page's Finance tab is the setup: linking, repairing, syncing and the reporting currency.
  • The command line. teanode finance, listed in the command line reference.
  • Your agent. Its finance tool. Ask "how much did we spend eating out in September?" or "are we on track for the emergency fund?"

Privacy

The rows are in your own PostgreSQL, next to your mail; nothing is sent to a finance service of ours, because there isn't one. The credentials a link keeps are sealed with the server's secret before they are stored. A SimpleFIN setup token or a credential you bring in is refused in conversation, because it would stay in the transcript and go to the model's provider; the agent tells you where to paste it instead.

What your agent reads about your money does go to the model you chose, the same as your mail. If that matters to you, the agent can run on a model on your own hardware.
