TeaNode

A personal agent for your whole life, on a server you own.

Your mail, calendar and contacts, your photos and where you have been, your health, your coding sessions, your meetings, your money and your passwords, joined up in one agent that sees how they connect. It all runs on your own hardware, one program and one PostgreSQL, so your life is stored with you and nowhere else. You choose the model, you choose what it may reach, and you can take any of it back. Point it at an Ollama or a vLLM of your own and nothing leaves the machine at all.

Everything it looks after

  • Chat. Talk to your agent, and to your people. One conversation with your agent on every page of the dashboard, in the iPhone app and on a call, with your family's and friends' chats beside it. Bring your agent into any of them with an @.
  • Search. One search across all of it. Type a word and your server looks in nine places at once: your conversations with your agent, mail, contacts, photos, memory, documents, recordings, places and finance. A few results from each, grouped, with a way into the full list. It asks no model and builds no new index.
  • Mail. Mail that sorts itself, and follows through. Your agent reads what arrives, marks what matters and what is waiting on you, keeps track of the bills, parcels and deadlines in it, and writes the replies for you to send. It is a whole mail server for your own domain, read here, in the app or in any mail program.
  • Calendar. A calendar that keeps up with you. Calendars and reminders on your server, in step with every device. Invitations that arrive as mail become events, your agent answers "am I free Thursday" and books the time, and a family calendar sits beside your own.
  • Contacts. An address book that knows who is who. The people you keep, with photographs, birthdays and addresses, in step with your phone and known to your agent, so "ask my father" or "Sam's new number" reaches the right person.
  • Photos. Your photo library, on your own server. Your iPhone backs its library up in the background, and the Photos page, the app and your agent all read the same library: by month, by album, by place and by the people in it. The faces are found on your server itself, never by a service elsewhere.
  • Location. Where you have been, kept by you. The iPhone app keeps your visits to places, also while it is closed, and your server names them and draws your day on a map. Share where you are with the people you choose, and ask your agent when you last went somewhere.
  • Health. Your health, on your server. The iPhone app copies your Apple Health data to your own server: steps, sleep, heart rate, workouts and the rest. The dashboard and the app chart it, and nobody else sees any of it unless you share it.
  • Coding. Your coding sessions, from anywhere. Keep your Claude Code and Codex sessions in herdr, and your agent sees every one of them on every computer you attached. Their questions come to you wherever you are, and each session starts knowing what your agent knows.
  • Knowledge. A memory that learns you. What your agent knows is a graph of pages, people, projects, places and things, joined by links that say how. Every fact is numbered, carries the words it came from and can be cited in an answer, and every change says who made it, so you can put it back.
  • Recordings. Meetings, written up for you. Record a meeting on your iPhone and your agent writes it up: a transcript as it happens, the speakers by name, a summary of what was decided, and what it taught filed in memory. The original stays on your server.
  • Finance. Your money, and your taxes. Link your banks, cards and brokerages through Plaid or SimpleFIN, or import a statement, and see where the money went, where the month is heading and what it all adds up to. The rows are kept in your own database, and your agent reads the same rows, so you can just ask.
  • Payments. Payments, with your word on each one. Your agent pays through a payment skill running on your own computer, such as Link, and never sees a card number. Lend it to somebody in your household with limits, and anything above them waits for you.
  • Passwords. Passwords your server cannot read. Logins, passkeys, one-time codes, cards and notes are sealed on your phone or in your browser before they reach your server, so neither the server nor whoever runs it can read them. Fill them in Chrome and on the iPhone, share them with your circle, and let your agent sign in with the few you choose.

Yours, and only yours

An agent that reaches every part of your life is only worth having if that life stays yours. TeaNode runs on hardware you own, keeps everything in your own database, and does nothing you have not turned on.

  • On your hardware. One program and one PostgreSQL on a machine you own. Your mail, photos, health and money are stored there, with no account with us at all, and your passwords are sealed on your own devices, so even the server cannot read them.
  • Your choice of model. A hosted model is sent only what a turn needs. Point it at an Ollama or a vLLM of your own and nothing leaves the machine at all.
  • Nothing without your say. Every part is off until you turn it on, the agent reaches only what you grant, and it stops for your word before anything it cannot undo.
  • Open, and yours to keep. MIT licensed, every line on GitHub. Back it up, export it, erase any of it, or switch it off.

TeaNode for iPhone

Chat with it, call it, answer it from a notification, and hand it what you are looking at from any app. One app for every TeaNode server: scan the code your dashboard shows and it connects as you, with nothing to type. It carries what the dashboard does, from mail and the calendar to photos, finance and knowledge, drawn in the system's own controls and at your text size.

Getting started

One command for just the assistant, and add your own mail when you want it.

  • Just the assistant. No domain and no mail ports. It listens on 127.0.0.1:8080; put an HTTPS proxy in front of it to reach it from your phone.
  • With your own mail. Mail at your domains, with the assistant and everything above.

Get started installs the assistant with one command, curl -fsSL https://teanode.com/get.sh | sh; the commands for both are in the Quick start.

What you need

  • A computer that stays on. The install command brings Docker on Linux, and PostgreSQL with it.
  • A model to think with. An API key, a ChatGPT plan you already pay for, or an Ollama or vLLM of your own.
  • For the iPhone app and for away from home, an HTTPS address in front of it, from any reverse proxy.
  • For your own mail, a domain whose DNS you can edit and ports 25, 80, 443, 587 and 993 open.
