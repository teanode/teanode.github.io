TeaNode

A personal assistant that lives on your own server.

It reads your mail, keeps your calendar, files your receipts and tax forms, writes up your meetings and keeps an eye on your coding sessions, and it remembers what it learns about you. Talk to it from the iPhone app, a browser or a call. One program and one PostgreSQL on your hardware; point it at an Ollama or a vLLM of your own and nothing leaves the machine.

What it looks after

  • Your mail. Sorts what arrives, says what is waiting on you, and drafts the answer in your voice. Sending stays with you.
  • Your days. A calendar, reminders, notes and an address book your phone keeps in step, and invitations answered from the conversation.
  • Your money. Banks, cards and brokerages linked or imported, spending filed your way, budgets that look ahead, and receipts matched to their charges.
  • Your taxes. Paystubs, W-2s, 1099s and 1098s read from a PDF or a photo, and the year added up into what you are likely to owe or get back.
  • Your meetings. Record on your iPhone, and get a transcript as it happens, the speakers by name, and a summary filed where it belongs.
  • What you told it. A memory of the people, projects and places in your life, which it writes as you talk, cites when it answers, and tidies in its dreams.
  • Your coding sessions. Claude Code and Codex on every computer. Their questions come to you, and they start knowing what your agent knows.
  • Your computer and browser. Files, a terminal and a browser on the machines you attach, with your word asked before anything it cannot undo.

TeaNode for iPhone

Chat with it, call it, answer it from a notification, and hand it what you are looking at from any app. One app for every TeaNode server: scan the code your dashboard shows and it connects as you, with nothing to type. It carries what the dashboard does, from mail and the calendar to finance and knowledge, drawn in the system's own controls and at your text size.

In TestFlight now, and on its way to the App Store.

Getting started

One compose file either way. Start with just the assistant, and add your own mail when you want it.

  • Just the assistant. No domain and no mail ports. It listens on 127.0.0.1:8080; put an HTTPS proxy in front of it to reach it from your phone.
  • With your own mail. Mail at your domains, with the assistant and everything above.

The commands for both are in the Quick start.

What you need

  • A computer that stays on, with Docker and compose. PostgreSQL comes with the compose file.
  • A model to think with. An API key, a ChatGPT plan you already pay for, or an Ollama or vLLM of your own.
  • For the iPhone app and for away from home, an HTTPS address in front of it, from any reverse proxy.
  • For your own mail, a domain whose DNS you can edit and ports 25, 80, 443, 587 and 993 open.
